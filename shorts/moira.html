Moira's Story

Moira was – is – a character I played in Daggerheart for a brief time. She was a witch who I conceived as effectively a broker of astrological favours, haunted by the terrible and magical choir of the firmament. This is a collection of vignettes showing her backstory.

She is sitting by a shy campfire, her bone crest like a halo in the dancing light. She wishes she could rest tonight, but the paths are closed and the doors are barred; she needs help, regardless of the price.

Tools of the trade: crystal, incense, sacred geometry. She feels the resonance of sky vibrating in her bones, pushes away the whispers of the moons, and there it is – the choir of stars. They see her, alien and curious, keen to know the request and to find a price. She hopes this will be a fair trade.

SURVIVAL IN EXCHANGE FOR MEMORY roars Tip of the Sword. None other offers a bargain. She freezes, weeps, and eventually agrees. Teenage indiscretions with a girl from her village, gone.

The power swells and she finds a direction. She will run and she will survive. There is a path that she did not see before, arduous and risky, but the stars will see her through it. The bargains are always kept, otherwise it's not good business for a witch.

The ship is unsteady, but the storm is no concern to any of them. They have spent long years at sea by now and so this weather is only an inconvenience. The roaring kraken however is a different story.

Men and women are scrambling for weapons; children and the elderly are hiding under the deck. If the monster prevails, it won't matter where you hide, but it is better to be out of the way of the warriors and witches.

The pouring rain is streaming down Moira's brow; her inky blue-black hair is sticking to her neck. The usual spells barely scorch the monster's skin, tentacles thick as boat hulls swinging around and picking off the fleet's warriors.

She falls back to the covered section of the deck. She has no need to draw the shapes anymore, they come to her on demand now. The crystals at her belt resonate obediently. The firmament listens. The stars are silent for several uncomfortable minutes.

THE BEAST FALLS/THE FLEET PAYS finally offers Third of Fox Tail, EVERY FIFTH/CHILD LEAVES. Moira freezes; this is not a twisted sacrifice - the children will live, but they will abandon the sea life. Cruel demand, for the fleets are already shrinking.

The kraken finally succeeds and climbs up on the bow of the ship. Across the length of the deck she is staring into its enormous beak; one of the tentacles throws a screaming man into the crushing maw. She shakes her head and looks down at the shining circle around her feet.

The bargain is made. In the coming months and years some of their youth will choose to leave forever. Lighting and thunder strike the kraken; it is not dead, but it is wounded. The warriors cheer and throw themselves at the beast with renewed vigour.

Moira feels power swelling through her and without thinking directs it out. Five, six - seven thunderstrikes converge on her, and through her body burst out as one towards the tentacled monster. The creature is torn to burning pieces, with the entire fleet watching.

There is much cheering that evening, but she is drinking alone to the side. No one dares ask the ship's witch what price she was asked to pay.
