Mermaid

I

Upon a new moon, I surface out of the depths draped in seaweed and adorned with pearls. Scales, metallic, black and blue and teal, sparkle with reflections of stars. I climb up the mossy pier, unseen but for a single cat.

I look towards the city lights. My sisters take to sailors, but the scent I've caught leads elsewhere.

I picture your face, and you are not the one I am promised to. Hunger and desire gush out of me like seawater. Like the tide, there is more than enough for everyone.

I seek you out in the streets and the bars, every step of borrowed feet bringing me closer. The scent grows stronger, and I abandon reason and care. My sisters call sailors to their doom, but you are calling me to mine.

I must feed tonight, sharp teeth and claws on your soft skin, tender lips, golden thigh.

I find you in a warmly lit place, hot with bodies. You are the sun itself, beautiful and terrible, radiant in the crowd. The waves of my heart crest and collapse. I cannot devour you; I sit and listen to your sweet voice until dawn yet again.

In a month I will return, hungry for your light once more.

II

Upon a new moon, I come out of the depths tangled in sea grass and wrapped in kelp. Scales, dull, black and blue and teal, barely catch the starlight. I climb up the concrete pier, ignored by the seagulls.

I look up at the city and throw up blood and seawater. My beloved under the waves nearly commanded me to head to the surface. The gnawing hunger has become impossible to ignore. I start forward without waiting until I can walk.

The scales itch and the seaweed trails, but I do not have the strength to clothe myself in a better disguise. I skulk between the shadows in the streets.

I picture your face again and for a moment I feel the warmth. I stumble — the borrowed feet fail and collapse into a tail. Devotion and desire flood the street like seawater.

I crawl onwards like a stranded beast. The place is near now, and there's few passersby this late.

The conversations die as I drag myself in, monstrous, aching, spiny; pale skin and dull scales, claws scratching wood and cloth. The smell of salt and kelp spills into the space. I realise I don't even know if you're here tonight.

I vomit more saltwater and blood.

Then I hear your birdsong, but I cannot make out the words.

You lean down and touch my dripping hair, hold my cheek and gently turn my head to face you. We lock gazes and —

I am lost and the sun burns my skin. Beyond the blaze of your eyes there is a hungering void, the darkest black I have ever seen. I stared it on all my my past visits. I am falling into this darkness forever.

A smile dances on your face, as if you have always known.

And you lean down, kiss my salt-coated lips, and I die a million deaths. Purest sunfire pours into my veins. Without thinking I reach for your hand and flinch as I expect the burn — and you gently grasp it.

In a month I will return, and I will drink the sun freely.

III

Upon a new moon, I find myself bathed in sweetwater and candlelight. Scales, glittering, black and blue and teal, reflect a warm glow for the first time. There are no creatures here to hide from.

I just about fit in the bath and my fins stick out of the water awkwardly - I pay no mind. My tail is coiled around and in between your legs. My hands are hungry for your hips, your back, your chest. My lips are soft on your cheek and my teeth are sharp on your neck.

It is like drinking the sun. It is like nothing I have ever done before.

Your palms against my writhing body, against my breasts, combing through my kelp-laced hair. I could have done this in a more familiar shape, but you wanted me here, in your bathroom. I remain unsure as to why. Is there novelty to this - or do you just like having a mermaid in your bathtub?

I might ask once my mouth isn't full of you.

I fight a part of me that wants to sink my fangs in your throat, tear your ribcage open and gorge on your heart while it's still beating. In this, I am what I always have been. But that would make it impossible to repeat this lovemaking, and that I would not endure.

You taste and smell of things I have little language for. It reminds me of the coastal winds in high summer.

You are like drinking the sun. You are so much like my beloved under the waves. I think you will one day enjoy each other.

I shift and uncoil. Kiss after kiss, I make my way down your chest. Your breathing gets heavier. The candles feel dimmed now. One more kiss on your mound; I wonder if I can reach to wrap my tail around your neck. I plunge further down and lose myself in a tide of delight.

It is a long night of feasting upon each other.
