Prayers to the Moon

  • Waxing and full and waning and new
  • A blossom, a fruit, a weave and a mirror
  • A lovesong, a lullaby, a dirge and an oath

These pieces were spurred by a Tumblr post by wordslikesilver, describing an addition to the classic neopagan lunar Triple Goddess - a fourth deity, the Sister, effectively a patron god of feminine sisterhood and of trans women.

She took up residence in my head and started growing, borrowing from Persephone and other underworld deities, becoming this strange hybrid of celestial and chthonic. I think a subversive goddess of defining your own fate and crossing gender boundaries would revel in that.

Prayer

  • On a spring morning I bring an offering of blossoms and smiles.
  • May the Maiden favour the bold and the dreaming.
  • May the sunrise be bright and clear.
  • On a summer noon I bring an offering of fruit and kisses.
  • May the Mother favour the loving and the caring.
  • May the midday sun fire the hearts.
  • On a autumn evening I bring an offering of bread and tears.
  • May the Crone favour the cunning and the mourning.
  • May the sunset be long and soothing.
  • Every night, I bring an offering of blood and oaths.
  • May the Sister cast her shadow upon us all. May we be her vigilant eyes, her ruthless mirror, her kind words, and her swift knife.
  • May the night be a tight embrace and a promise kept.

Ritual

In the dead of night, in a dim bedroom, I wipe the tears away and light the incense.

We don't invoke her name often - not for fear of divine retribution. Offerings are willingly made and they come with no requests. Pleas for help are only for the direst of times, when the world turns against us with sharp swords and cruel words.

The heavy smell of the incense, the dancing light of a candle. The distorted reflection of a mirror, the sharp cut of a dagger. Blood, lipstick, wine, pomegranate. Then, a poem. I could never bring her a church prayer, I will sing the oldest song I know. I trust in my heart that this she will enjoy.

‘Ud ria, ud surá ria…’

A remembrance of a past before the past, opening a story of valiant deed and immeasurable loss, a language dead since before this city was even a village.

On a night like this, I know you are somewhere else, doing a similar rite. You are calming your racing heart and steeling yourself for a long fight.

A time will come when we will be doing this together, you and I, and with others too. We will hold each other tightly. We will be making the offering of song and poetry in gratitude. We will pick verses of love and joy. We will be feasting on fruit and wine, becoming what she calls us to be.

Tonight is a new moon, I am praying to the Sister.

  • One with sharp teeth, lend me your wrath in protection of my sisters.
  • One with radiant gaze, lend me your devotion in service of my lovers.
  • One with no thread, lend me your divinity in governance of my fate.
  • Take my words, take my fruit, take my blood, so that our promises are kept.
