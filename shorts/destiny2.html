Tribute to Destiny 2

The wind was howling, whistling between the rotting girders and detaching panels of the old colony. Snow like razorblades was cutting nearly horizontally. She wasn't bothered, her metal and synthetic tuned down to ignore the blizzard.

It had been a few years since she came here. The pyramids had laid dormant, the pirates abandoned their city, and the coppery machines had kept to themselves these days.

Europa was quiet once again, save for the snowstorms.

She wasn't here for the fight, or to uncover mysteries. She was here for a memory.

 

Cassandra-5 walked through the icicle-lined doorway and into the laboratory she was at least once reborn.

She strolled the deathly quiet hallways, orange and white, strewn with discarded equipment and an occasional corpse of an intruder. Over the years they had to defend this complex so many times; the old bastard tried to blow them all up while they did that, even though they were protecting his own secrets.

Even though ultimately it was he that gave her the second chance centuries ago, she never liked or trusted the asshole.

She turned a corner and there it was; vast hall, an archive, servers like columns and walls spanning multiple floors, stretching into a hazy distance deep beneath the glacier. Her memory was somewhere here.

The console at the centre was active; it wasn't even secured. Cassandra-5 knew this deep into the complex there was simply no need to defend it. If anyone made it that far, they have already passed most of the treasures anyway.

Query: exomind upload records, subject Cassandra-5

Response was a location. She moved on.

 

The archive was a labyrinth, but one with a plan, a logic, and a pattern. The data storage she was looking for was exactly where the master record told her it would be. A single drive in an array, in a crypt full of billions of server racks.

With a trembling hand - it shouldn't tremble, it was ceramic and composite - she lifted the drive out of the enclosure and plugged into it. And there they were: Cassandra 1 through to 4, and then the one before them all.

'Alright, kid, you better be sure,' said a calm man in the perfectly preserved memory. 'You know there are other ways, maybe a bit slower, but you will still get... what you need?'

'Yes,' a boy's voice - she choked up. 'And the other ways won't fix the other problems I have.'

'Alright then,' smiled the man, 'I will see you on the other side, sister.'

The records continued playing and copying into her own mind, but she wasn't listening. She ignored the risk of memory overload forcing another reset, the number behind her name going up, her life getting wiped out again. She sobbed on the floor of the archive for hours, a dry dirge of a synthetic body, not one tear rolling down her white ceramic cheek.

 

She walked out of the complex a few hours later. The blizzard had cleared, though the air remained deathly cold. Europa had no kindness for the living. An icy tomb for thousands ever since man set foot on this globe.

Cassandra-5 surveyed the gleaming white glacier, the decaying city on the horizon, and the looming black pyramid in the other direction.

'Are you... are you okay?' Diana unfolded like a papercraft flower from a netherspace. 'This looked... harrowing.'

Cassandra looked back at her Ghost, and weighted up her answer.

'Yes,' she whispered and smiled gently. 'I have finally all the pieces.'

They both looked up at the sky, Jupiter hanging heavy above them. A few minutes passed, debris from the collapsed orbital station raining across the sky like shooting stars.

'Would you like to go somewhere... new?' offered Cassandra.

'I thought you'd never ask,' chirped Diana, her shell spinning with excitement.

Games have great power in them.

When an old man hosting an illicit bloodsport called me sister, something in me started breaking. It took a few more years before another book, a song, a movie, another realisation, talking to a friend, all combined to make me take my fate in my own hands.

Destiny 2 gave me and my friends a light in a time when the world seemed too dark. It gave us a hangout when we were all locked up in our homes. It inspired me to make my own things.

Ultimately we all walked away from it when it stopped being fun; but in my heart and my memory this game will always be special. I wish it could have gone out on a higher note than it did.

See you in the stars, Guardians.
